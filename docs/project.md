# The UVAR Box project

<span class="page-kicker">European preparatory action · 2020–2022</span>

UVAR Box was an EU-funded project that investigated how cities and other responsible authorities could make information about **urban vehicle access regulations (UVARs)** available in a structured, machine-readable way.

Its intended beneficiaries included local authorities, National Access Points (NAPs), mobility information providers and, ultimately, road users who need to understand access restrictions before or during a journey.

The original user journey is explained in [How the tool worked](/tool). The dated figures and coverage are examined in [2022 pilot results](/results).

The project ran during 2020–2022 under European Commission contract **MOVE/B4/SER/2019-498/SI2.832125**. ARMIS coordinated the consortium. Its five pilot Member States were **Austria, Belgium, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands**; additional work included other countries, such as Portugal.

## What are UVARs?

UVARs govern when and under what conditions vehicles can enter, use or park in particular urban areas. Examples include:

- **Low-emission zones**, which restrict access based on vehicle emissions.
- **Limited traffic zones**, which may use permits or other access conditions.
- **Parking regulations**, which can limit where or when vehicles may park.
- **Congestion charging schemes** and **pedestrian zones**.

These schemes are decided and maintained by the competent authorities. This website does not provide binding interpretations of any city's current rules.

## What UVAR Box set out to do

The work connected three practical needs:

1. **Define a shared data model.** Describe UVAR conditions consistently using a DATEX II-based structure.
2. **Support digitisation.** Develop a tool and guidance for authorities to create or convert structured UVAR information.
3. **Enable onward information provision.** Examine how data could be accessed through NAPs and used in services for road users.

This model was intended to reduce repeated data conversion and enable interoperability across national and local systems. It was a **project approach**, not a guarantee that every city or information service adopted it.

## Who was UVAR Box designed for?

The original project described four practical scenarios. They illustrate **intended uses**, not evidence that each service was deployed:

- **Visiting drivers:** receive understandable information about an access zone in a journey-planning or navigation service before reaching it.
- **Urban freight and logistics operators:** avoid repeatedly collecting and translating local restrictions for different deliveries and vehicle types.
- **National Access Point teams:** receive consistently described restrictions from authorities, with clearer responsibilities for publication and updates.
- **Navigation and mobility-service developers:** work with a common machine-readable format instead of interpreting separate city-specific data formats.

Digitisation also depended on people and coordination. The project's **country coaches** supported exchanges between cities, regional or national authorities and the technical team, taking account of differing administrative procedures and languages. This support was part of the **2020–2022 project**, not a current onboarding service.

## What the 2022 project delivered

The Final Report, available in its official EU edition published in 2025, describes the DATEX II UVAR data model, a digitisation tool, training and country-coach activities, and more than 500 digitised UVAR entries during the 2022 pilot. Its detailed table records 570 entries across several regulation types at project completion.

The project also studied differences in legal and organisational arrangements, data-access processes and potential use by navigation and information services.

![UVAR Box project mark](/brand/uvarbox-original.png)

The number reported in 2022 is **not** a count of active, validated UVARs in 2026. The availability and licensing of any dataset intended for new public distribution require separate confirmation.

## What came next: UVAR Exchange

UVAR Exchange was a separate, subsequent European Parliament preparatory action commissioned by DG MOVE and led by Panteia. It began in September 2021, overlapping with UVAR Box, and presented findings at a final stakeholder workshop in Brussels on 18 October 2022.

It complemented the digitisation work in two areas: **communicating restrictions to drivers** through physical signs, variable message signs and cooperative intelligent transport systems (C-ITS); and **examining cross-border exchange of vehicle and driver information** relevant to enforcement. Its scope and results should not be confused with the separate modernisation ideas being discussed for 2027.

For contemporary public accounts, see the [European Commission's project announcement (September 2021)](https://urban-mobility-observatory.transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/uvarexchange-project-kicks-2021-09-24_en) and the [POLIS final stakeholder workshop (October 2022)](https://www.polisnetwork.eu/event/uvar-exchange-final-stakeholder-workshop/).

## European policy context

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/670 addresses accessibility, exchange and reuse of certain road-traffic data, including relevant regulations and restrictions. Article 5 specifies DATEX II or TN-ITS formats for the data within its scope. The regulation's applicability and transitional provisions must be read together with the Annex; this site is not a legal compliance assessment for a particular authority.

For authoritative wording, consult [the regulation on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2022/670/oj/eng) and the [Commission's UVAR overview](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/urban-transport/urban-vehicle-access-regulations_en).

## Why the work remains relevant

Authorities publish regulations for specific places, vehicles and time periods. Service providers need consistent and up-to-date data to communicate those rules to travellers. The European Commission's [National Access Points overview](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/smart-mobility/road/its-directive-and-action-plan/national-access-points_en) explains the role of NAPs in facilitating discovery, exchange and reuse of transport data.

The UVAR Box report recommended continued work on data maintenance, harmonised exchange and cooperation between cities, NAPs and service providers. Those are historical recommendations; they should not be read as a statement that a specific current implementation has been delivered.
